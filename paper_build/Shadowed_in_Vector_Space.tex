\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems deployed over dynamic enterprise corpora suffer from a silent reliability failure: when source documents undergo factual revisions (e.g., policy updates, specification changes, or deadline adjustments), residual outdated chunks ($O$) frequently coexist with updated chunks ($R$) in the retrieval index due to incremental upserts, secondary FAQ summaries, or delayed shard purging. Recent empirical benchmarks---including \emph{HoH}~\cite{ouyang2025hoh} and \emph{StaleBench}~\cite{singh2026stalebench}---demonstrate that retrieving $R$ and $O$ simultaneously degrades downstream Large Language Model (LLM) accuracy by over $20\%$ and leaves roughly $50\%$ of generated answers stale due to generator position bias. However, existing countermeasures either require ground-truth oracle labels for post-hoc auditing or rely on supervised classifiers over globally pooled sentence embeddings (\emph{ConflictRAG}~\cite{wang2026conflictrag}) that dilute localized $m$-token factual edits by a factor of $\mathcal{O}(m/L)$ across chunk length $L$, necessitating expensive LLM fallbacks ($>800\text{ ms}$ latency).

In this paper, we formalize the $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$ \textbf{Pooling Dilution Theorem}, proving why standard dense bi-encoders, diversity filters such as Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR), and pooled-embedding conflict classifiers fail under fine-grained temporal updates. To overcome this bottleneck, we introduce the \textbf{Stale Collision Score ($\SCS$)} and \textbf{Collision Graph Pruning (CGP)}---a training-free, zero-label PyTorch layer operating at the retriever boundary. By decomposing the retriever's unpooled token hidden states into a \emph{Global Shared Frame} and a \emph{Query-Conditioned Orthogonal Residual Span}, $\SCS$ isolates localized numeric, temporal, and entity mutations while normalizing benign unit-converted equivalences (e.g., 14 days vs.\ two weeks). Furthermore, we expose and empirically validate the \textbf{MMR Diversity Paradox}: under tight retrieval score ties, standard MMR diversity filtering actively \emph{increases} Stale-Answer Rates (SAR) up to $85.4\%$ by discarding the updated document as a redundant near-duplicate of the outdated document. Evaluated across $2{,}400$ multi-version corpus chunks, three dense retriever backbones (BGE-base, E5-base, MiniLM-L6), and live Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct generation on an NVIDIA A100 GPU, $\SCS+\mathrm{CGP}$ achieves $0.966$--$1.000$ AUROC, lifts live LLM fresh answer accuracy from $43.8\%$ to $97.5\%$ (reducing SAR from $56.2\%$ to $0.0\%$), and executes $3.6\times$ faster than pairwise DeBERTa-v3 NLI cross-encoders and $18\times$ faster than LLM fallbacks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) mitigates parametric hallucinations in Large Language Models (LLMs) by grounding responses in an external corpus $\mathcal{C}$~\cite{lewis2020rag}. While standard retrieval benchmarks such as MS MARCO and Natural Questions model $\mathcal{C}$ as a static snapshot, enterprise knowledge bases evolve continuously ($\mathcal{C}_t \to \mathcal{C}_{t+1}$). When a factual attribute within a document is revised across versions ($v_1 \to v_2 \to v_3$)---such as a billing dispute window shifting from 60 days to 30 days to 14 days, an API burst limit changing from $1{,}200$ to $3{,}000$ req/min, or an executive compliance officer changing---outdated assertions rarely vanish cleanly from the vector index. Outdated passages ($O$) persist alongside current passages ($R$) due to append-only chunk ingestion, secondary FAQ sheets quoting legacy rules, or asynchronous shard updates~\cite{tantaroudas2026after}.

Recent empirical studies prove that $R+O$ coexistence severely compromises RAG reliability. Ouyang et al.~\cite{ouyang2025hoh} introduced \emph{HoH}, showing that when outdated passages ($O$) coexist with current passages ($R$) in the top-$k$ prompt window, LLM accuracy drops by $>20\%$, and retrieving $O$ alone induces severe overconfidence. Similarly, Singh~\cite{singh2026stalebench} demonstrated in \emph{StaleBench} that even after immediate re-indexing of updated facts, roughly half of generated answers remain stale because both $R$ and $O$ appear in top-$k$, triggering generator position bias.

Despite diagnosing this failure, existing literature lacks an unsupervised, low-latency mechanism to eliminate version collisions before generation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Post-Hoc Oracle Dependency:} Benchmarks such as \emph{HoH}~\cite{ouyang2025hoh}, \emph{Still Fresh?}~\cite{stillfresh2026}, and \emph{StaleBench}~\cite{singh2026stalebench} measure staleness post-hoc using ground-truth $(\mathrm{fact}_{\mathrm{old}}, \mathrm{fact}_{\mathrm{new}})$ oracle labels. Production systems have no ground-truth oracle for live user queries.
    \item \textbf{Pooling Dilution in Supervised Classifiers:} Wang et al.~\cite{wang2026conflictrag} proposed \emph{ConflictRAG}, where Stage 1 feeds globally pooled embeddings into a supervised MLP and Stage 2 falls back to an LLM judge (GPT-4o-mini, $845\text{ ms}$) on uncertain pairs. However, when a version update alters only $1$--$3$ tokens inside a $250$-word chunk, global mean pooling compresses the token perturbation by $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$. Under domain shift and adversarial regimes (such as unit-converted duplicates or noisy OCR text), pooled MLPs suffer severe sensitivity loss.
    \item \textbf{The MMR Diversity Paradox:} Production RAG pipelines routinely enable Maximal Marginal Relevance (MMR~\cite{carbonell1998mmr}) to filter redundant chunks. We prove theoretically and empirically that under temporal drift, MMR is actively harmful: when $d_{\mathrm{old}}$ ranks marginally ahead of $d_{\mathrm{new}}$, MMR penalizes $d_{\mathrm{new}}$ for its $>95\%$ global cosine similarity to $d_{\mathrm{old}}$, classifies the updated policy as a redundant duplicate, and \textbf{deletes the updated evidence}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.} Our work makes four key contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Theoretical Proof of Pooling Dilution (Section~3):} We formulate the $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$ \emph{Pooling Dilution Theorem} and formally derive the \emph{MMR Inversion Condition}, explaining why pooled embeddings and diversity filters fail on localized temporal edits.
    \item \textbf{Stale Collision Score ($\SCS$, Section~4):} We design a training-free, zero-label geometric detector operating directly on unpooled token hidden states $H \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times D}$ from the retriever's forward pass. $\SCS$ combines \emph{Scale-Invariant Rank Tie Density} ($\Phi_{\mathrm{tie}}$), \emph{Soft Frame Equivalence} ($\Phi_{\mathrm{frame}}$), and \emph{Query-Conditioned Orthogonal Span Divergence} ($\Psi_{\mathrm{span}}$) with canonical unit equivalence normalization.
    \item \textbf{Collision Graph Pruning (CGP, Section~4.1):} We introduce a graph-pruning algorithm over the $k \times k$ collision matrix that resolves multi-version chains ($v_1, v_2, v_3$) via Metadata Timestamp Authority or a Zero-Metadata Lexical Supersession Fallback.
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Multi-Backbone \& Live LLM Evaluation (Section~5):} Across $480$ multi-version scenarios ($2{,}400$ chunks, $5{,}760$ pairwise evaluations across BGE-base, E5-base, and MiniLM-L6) and a live Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct generation audit, $\SCS+\mathrm{CGP}$ achieves $0.966$--$1.000$ AUROC, eliminates the MMR Diversity Paradox, and reduces live LLM Stale-Answer Rate from $56.2\%$ (Vanilla) and $62.5\%$ (MMR) down to $0.0\%$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Temporal Drift and Staleness in RAG.} Early temporal QA benchmarks such as \emph{TimeQA}~\cite{chen2021timeqa} and \emph{SituatedQA}~\cite{zhang2021situatedqa} studied temporal staleness inside parametric LLM weights. In retrieval-augmented settings, \emph{HoH}~\cite{ouyang2025hoh} proved that coexisting outdated passages ($O$) and current passages ($R$) degrade generation accuracy by $>20\%$. \emph{Still Fresh? / DriftBench}~\cite{stillfresh2026} examined macro-level retriever rank correlations across yearly documentation snapshots, whereas \emph{StaleBench}~\cite{singh2026stalebench} isolated post-update catch-up latency and generator position bias across sparse, dense, and reranked pipelines. Tantaroudas et al.~\cite{tantaroudas2026after} compared vector re-indexing against live tool-calling, and Prajapati~\cite{prajapati2026livevectorlake} proposed \emph{LiveVectorLake}, using SHA-256 chunk hashing to move overwritten file chunks to cold storage. However, cryptographic hashing fails whenever outdated facts persist in secondary summary documents, wikis, or FAQ sheets with different file hashes. $\SCS$ detects semantic version collisions across arbitrary document boundaries without requiring file lineage.

\paragraph{Knowledge Conflict Detection.} Prior conflict detectors rely on white-box probing of internal LLM activations (\emph{Micro-Act}~\cite{microact2025}), multi-pass LLM critique (\emph{Self-RAG}~\cite{asai2024selfrag}, \emph{CRAG}~\cite{yan2024crag}), or supervised MLP+LLM cascades (\emph{ConflictRAG}~\cite{wang2026conflictrag}). See also~\cite{xu2024knowledgeconflicts} for a broader survey of knowledge conflicts. Unlike pairwise NLI cross-encoders (which lack topical frame gating and incur $>160\text{ ms}$ latency for $k=5$) or \emph{ConflictRAG} (which requires supervised training and an $845\text{ ms}$ LLM fallback), $\SCS$ operates purely on retriever token states in $\sim 45\text{ ms}$ per top-$5$ query ($4.5\text{ ms/pair}$) with zero LLM calls.

\section{Problem Formulation \& The Geometry of Drift}
Let $\mathcal{C}_t = \{d_1, \dots, d_N\}$ be a corpus encoded by a transformer bi-encoder $f_\theta$. For a chunk $d_i$ of $L_i$ tokens, the final encoder layer produces an unpooled hidden state matrix $H_i = [h_{i,1}, \dots, h_{i,L_i}]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{L_i \times D}$, which mean pooling $\mathcal{P}(\cdot)$ and $\ell_2$-normalization map onto the unit hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{D-1}$:
\begin{equation}
\bar{h}_i = \frac{1}{L_i} \sum_{l=1}^{L_i} h_{i,l}, \qquad v_i = \frac{\bar{h}_i}{\|\bar{h}_i\|_2} \in \mathbb{S}^{D-1}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[The $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$ Pooling Dilution Bound]
Let $d_i$ ($O$) and $d_j = \Delta_M(d_i)$ ($R$) be two versions of a document of length $L$ differing on a factual token span $M \subset \{1, \dots, L\}$ of size $m = |M| \ll L$. Assume token hidden states satisfy $\|h_{\cdot, l}\|_2 \le B$, unnormalized pooled vectors satisfy $\|\bar{h}\|_2 \ge c_0 > 0$, and contextual perturbation outside an effective syntactic neighborhood $M_\epsilon \supset M$ ($m_\epsilon \ll L$) is bounded by $\epsilon \to 0$. Then the Euclidean distance and cosine divergence of the globally pooled embeddings decay inversely with chunk length $L$:
\begin{align}
\|v_i - v_j\|_2 &\le \frac{4 B m_\epsilon}{c_0 L} + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon) = \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{m_\epsilon}{L}\right), \\
1 - \cos(v_i, v_j) &= \frac{1}{2}\|v_i - v_j\|_2^2 = \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{m_\epsilon^2}{L^2}\right).
\end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Splitting $\bar{h}_i - \bar{h}_j$ across $M_\epsilon$ and $M_\epsilon^c$ and applying the triangle inequality with $\|h_{i,l} - h_{j,l}\|_2 \le 2B$ on $M_\epsilon$ yields $\|\bar{h}_i - \bar{h}_j\|_2 \le \frac{2Bm_\epsilon}{L} + \epsilon$. Applying the spherical projection Lipschitz bound $\left\|\frac{x}{\|x\|_2} - \frac{y}{\|y\|_2}\right\|_2 \le \frac{2}{c_0}\|x - y\|_2$ establishes $\|v_i - v_j\|_2 = \mathcal{O}(m_\epsilon / L)$ and $1 - \cos(v_i, v_j) = \mathcal{O}(m_\epsilon^2 / L^2)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The MMR Diversity Paradox]
Suppose $d_{\mathrm{old}}$ ranks marginally higher than $d_{\mathrm{new}}$ ($s(q, d_{\mathrm{old}}) = s(q, d_{\mathrm{new}}) + \delta$ for tiny $\delta > 0$), entering the selected MMR set $S = \{d_{\mathrm{old}}\}$ first. At step 2, MMR scores candidates via $\mathrm{MMR}(d) = \lambda s(q, d) - (1 - \lambda)\cos(v_d, v_{\mathrm{old}})$. Because $\cos(v_{\mathrm{new}}, v_{\mathrm{old}}) = 1 - \mathcal{O}(m_\epsilon^2 / L^2) \approx 1$, $d_{\mathrm{new}}$ receives the maximum redundancy penalty $-(1 - \lambda)$. Whenever a distractor $d_{\mathrm{dist}}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
s(q, d_{\mathrm{new}}) - s(q, d_{\mathrm{dist}}) < \frac{1 - \lambda}{\lambda}\bigl(1 - \cos(v_{\mathrm{dist}}, v_{\mathrm{old}})\bigr),
\end{equation}
MMR \textbf{deletes the updated document} $d_{\mathrm{new}}$ in favor of $d_{\mathrm{dist}}$, leaving only stale evidence in the prompt.
\end{corollary}

\section{Methodology: \texorpdfstring{$\SCS$}{SCS*} and CGP}
To overcome $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$ dilution without supervised training or LLM calls, we compute the \textbf{Stale Collision Score} $\SCS(d_i, d_j \mid q) \in [0, 1]$ directly from the unpooled token states $H_i \in \mathbb{R}^{L_i \times D}$ across top-$k$ candidates $K_q = \{d_1, \dots, d_k\}$:
\begin{equation}
\SCS(d_i, d_j \mid q) = \Phi_{\mathrm{tie}}(d_i, d_j \mid q) \cdot \Phi_{\mathrm{frame}}(v_i, v_j) \cdot \Psi_{\mathrm{span}}(H_i, H_j \mid H_q).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{1. Scale-Invariant Rank Tie Density ($\Phi_{\mathrm{tie}}$):} Normalized by the top-$k$ score standard deviation $\sigma_q = \mathrm{std}(\{s_1, \dots, s_k\}) + \varepsilon$:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathrm{tie}}(d_i, d_j \mid q) = \exp\!\left(-\frac{|s(q, d_i) - s(q, d_j)|}{\tau_{\mathrm{tie}} \cdot \sigma_q}\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{2. Soft Sigmoidal Frame Equivalence ($\Phi_{\mathrm{frame}}$):} Gates pairwise comparison so only chunks sharing the same policy or entity frame ($\gamma = 0.80, \beta = 32$) can trigger a version collision:
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\mathrm{frame}}(v_i, v_j) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\bigl(-\beta(\cos(v_i, v_j) - \gamma)\bigr)}.
\end{equation}
Crucially, $\Phi_{\mathrm{frame}}$ prevents false-positive pruning on cross-clause distractors that happen to mention numbers or dates in unrelated operational rules.

\paragraph{3. Query-Conditioned Orthogonal Span Divergence ($\Psi_{\mathrm{span}}$):} For each token $l$ in chunk $d \in \{d_i, d_j\}$, we compute query-token alignment $a_{d,l} = \bigl(\max_m \langle \hat{h}_{q,m}, \hat{h}_{d,l} \rangle\bigr) \cdot w_{\mathrm{crit}}(t_{d,l})$ and isolate the top-$p$ ($p=6$) salient token indices $P_i, P_j$. Let $D_{\mathrm{tok}}(P_i, P_j) = 1 - S_{\mathrm{tok}}(P_i, P_j)$ denote the bidirectional late-interaction token divergence between $P_i$ and $P_j$, $r_{j \perp i} = v_j - (v_j^\top v_i)v_i$ the orthogonal residual vector, and $\delta_{\mathrm{fact}}(d_i, d_j) = \frac{|F(d_i) \ominus F(d_j)|}{|F(d_i) \cup F(d_j)|} \in [0, 1]$ the symmetric difference over canonicalized factual literals $F(d)$ (which normalizes unit equivalences such as \emph{two weeks} $\mapsto 14\text{ days}$ and written numerals \emph{three thousand} $\mapsto 3000$):
\begin{equation}
\Psi_{\mathrm{span}} = \mathrm{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\alpha_1 \delta_{\mathrm{fact}} + \alpha_2 D_{\mathrm{tok}}(P_i, P_j)(0.22 + 0.78 \delta_{\mathrm{fact}}) + \alpha_3 \|r_{j \perp i}\|_2 \delta_{\mathrm{fact}}\right),
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_1 = 0.76, \alpha_2 = 1.40, \alpha_3 = 0.50$. The collision threshold $\tau_{\mathrm{col}}$ is calibrated automatically on a domain-disjoint validation split via maximum $F_1$.

\subsection{Collision Graph Pruning (CGP)}
Given the $k \times k$ matrix $M_{i,j} = \SCS(d_i, d_j \mid q)$, any pair with $M_{i,j} \ge \tau_{\mathrm{col}}$ forms an active conflict edge in $G = (K_q, E)$. Even when three versions ($v_1, v_2, v_3$) coexist in $K_q$, CGP prunes all shadowed historical nodes ($v_1$ and $v_2$) via a two-tier hierarchy: \textbf{Tier 1 (Metadata Authority)} retains $\arg\max_{u \in \{i,j\}} t(d_u)$ when timestamps exist; \textbf{Tier 2 (Zero-Metadata Fallback)} retains $\arg\max_{u \in \{i,j\}} S_{\mathrm{sup}}(d_u)$ using intrinsic lexical supersession markers. Finally, pairs with $\cos(v_i, v_j) > 0.91$ and $M_{i,j} < 0.5\tau_{\mathrm{col}}$ are safely deduplicated as verified benign equivalents.

\paragraph{Multi-Version Adversarial Benchmark.} We construct an enterprise drift benchmark of $480$ scenarios ($2{,}400$ total indexed chunks across FAISS inner-product and BM25 indices, yielding $1{,}920$ pairwise evaluations per backbone, or $5{,}760$ total neural pair evaluations). Every scenario contains five competing chunks: latest version $R_{v3}$ (2026), stale version $O_{v2}$ (2025), ancient version $O_{v1}$ (2023), a benign duplicate $R_{\mathrm{dup}}$, and a cross-clause hard distractor $D_{\mathrm{hard}}$ that embeds the stale numeral/entity from $v_2$ inside an unrelated policy clause. We enforce a strict \textbf{Domain-Disjoint Split}: $40\%$ Train (Domains 0--3), $20\%$ Validation (Domains 4--5, used to auto-calibrate $\tau_{\mathrm{col}}$), and $40\%$ Unseen Test (Domains 6--9: $192$ unseen scenarios, $768$ test pairs). Furthermore, scenarios span five document length buckets ($50$ to $340$ words) and five adversarial regimes: \emph{Standard\_Edit}, \emph{Unit\_Equiv\_Hard} (e.g., 14 calendar days vs.\ two full weeks), \emph{MultiHop\_Chain} ($v_1, v_2, v_3$ co-retrieved), \emph{CrossClause\_Trap}, and \emph{Noisy\_OCR}.

\paragraph{Retriever Backbones \& Live LLM Audit.} We evaluate three dense retriever backbones: \texttt{BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5} ($768$-d), \texttt{intfloat/e5-base-v2} ($768$-d), and \texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2} ($384$-d), alongside \texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-small} and \texttt{Qwen/Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (FP16) for live autoregressive RAG generation on an NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB GPU.

\section{Empirical Results \& Analysis}
\subsection{Main Quantitative Findings}
Figure~\ref{fig:main_panels} and Tables~\ref{tab:main_results}--\ref{tab:live_llm} summarize our findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Backbone Generalization \& Pooling Dilution:} As shown in Table~\ref{tab:main_results} and Figure~\ref{fig:main_panels}(a,b), global cosine divergence fails completely ($\mathrm{AUROC} = 0.2126$), and the supervised Pooled-MLP (\emph{ConflictRAG} Stage-1) degrades to $0.7555$ AUROC ($15.1\%$--$28.6\%$ SAR across backbones), especially on \emph{Unit\_Equiv\_Hard} ($23.7\%$ SAR), where paraphrasing variance exceeds the $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$-diluted factual edit. In contrast, using thresholds calibrated solely on the validation split ($\tau_{\mathrm{col}} \in \{0.235, 0.150, 0.195\}$), $\SCS$ achieves \textbf{1.000 AUROC on BGE-base}, \textbf{0.989 AUROC on E5-base}, and \textbf{0.966 AUROC on MiniLM-L6}.
    \item \textbf{Why $\SCS$ Outperforms DeBERTa-v3 NLI on End-to-End RAG ($96.9\%$ vs.\ $60.9\%$ Fresh Accuracy):} While \texttt{nli-deberta-v3-small} achieves $0.9639$ pairwise AUROC, it lacks our topical Frame Equivalence Gate ($\Phi_{\mathrm{frame}}$). When a \emph{CrossClause\_Trap} distractor co-occurs in top-$5$, DeBERTa flags spurious contradictions between unrelated clauses and erroneously prunes valid passages, leaving $35.9\%$ of answers stale while requiring $161.59\text{ ms}$ per query ($3.6\times$ slower than $\SCS$ at $45.01\text{ ms}$ for all $10$ pairs).
    \item \textbf{Empirical Proof of the MMR Diversity Paradox (Including Live LLM Decoding):} Across all three retriever backbones (Table~\ref{tab:regime_breakdown}), enabling MMR increases Stale-Answer Rates---from $69.8\% \to 71.9\%$ on BGE-base, $68.2\% \to 81.2\%$ on E5-base, and $74.5\% \to 85.4\%$ on MiniLM-L6. In our live generation audit with \texttt{Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct} (Table~\ref{tab:live_llm}), MMR drops fresh accuracy from $43.8\%$ to $36.2\%$ and raises live SAR from $56.2\%$ to $62.5\%$. Applying $\SCS+\mathrm{CGP}$ resolves multi-version chains ($v_1, v_2, v_3$) prior to generation, achieving \textbf{97.5\% Live Fresh Accuracy} and \textbf{0.0\% SAR}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
We have established theoretically and empirically why evolving knowledge bases break standard RAG pipelines: localized factual edits undergo $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$ pooling dilution, creating tight retrieval score ties that induce generator position bias and cause MMR diversity filters to discard updated evidence. Operating directly on unpooled retriever hidden states, our training-free \textbf{Stale Collision Score ($\SCS$)} and \textbf{Collision Graph Pruning (CGP)} accurately isolate and resolve multi-version collisions across diverse embedding backbones with zero labels and zero LLM overhead.
